Snapshots

The snapshot backend snapshots builds the climate from a reference snapshot (e.g. present day) plus the terms of [forcing]: each a coefficient, a scalar of the forcing time (e.g. a glacial index), times a pattern, the difference of two snapshots (e.g. LGM minus pre-industrial) or a uniform anomaly (see yelmox), on the fesm-utils varslice reader and tsgen. It supplies the atmosphere (monthly tas, pr, lapse-rate corrected to the current surface) and the ocean as depth profiles (to_ann, so_ann), which the marine shelf interpolates to the shelf base. It runs on grid_clim, reads [snapshots] and the [snapshot_<name>] groups, and also supplies the ocean of the rembo climate (an ocean-only reference snapshot).

  • Program: yelmox (make yelmox), backend in libs/snapshots.f90, behind libs/yelmox_climate.f90.
  • Select: [comps] climate = "snapshots".
  • Output: with [output] write_clim, snap.nc, the climate as applied (after the transforms): the annual air temperature and its summer mean (t2m_ann, t2m_sum), the annual precipitation, the ocean profiles (to_ann, so_ann on depth), the value of each term’s coefficient (term_<name>), the surface elevation, the monthly atmosphere (tas, pr), tsl_ann and the diagnostics of the active transforms.

The transforms [taper] and [bias] (see yelmox) apply after the terms, in this order.

Configuration

Example: yelmox/yelmox_Greenland.nml, present day plus the PMIP3 LGM minus piControl anomaly weighted by alpha (here the orbital record r2020), the ocean plus a quarter of the mean air temperature anomaly (the derived coefficient dTo):

&forcing
    reference = "pd"                  ! [snapshot_pd], in the par file
    coefs     = "alpha" "dTo"         ! [coef_alpha] (par file: alpha_orbital_r2020), [coef_dTo] (library)
    terms     = "lgm" "ocean"         ! [term_lgm]: picontrol -> lgm on tas, pr; [term_ocean]: dTo on to
/

&snapshots
    lapse     = 0.0080 0.0065         ! [K m-1] annual, summer (default)
    f_p       = 0.06                  ! [K-1] precipitation scaling (default 0.05)
/

The snapshots of the run are the reference and the snap0/snap1 of its terms (no list): pd, picontrol and lgm here. Every key of [snapshots] and [snapshot_<name>] has a default in input/yelmox_defaults.nml (see Defaults): combine = "anomaly" (snapshots as anomalies from the reference), fields = "tas" "pr" "to" "so" "zs" (the fields a snapshot may bind), grid_global = "GLOBAL-1DEG", … Two kinds of group:

  • Rules, [snapshots]: the lapse rates lapse (annual, summer) and the precipitation sensitivity f_p (β_p), used in the sea-level reduction of the snapshots, the uniform terms and the move to the model surface.
  • Snapshots, [snapshot_<name>]: exp (the experiment, {exp} in the database paths), monthly (default True), clim_sigma/ocn_sigma ([km] Gaussian smoothing of the atmosphere and the ocean at loading, the land mask following the smoothed elevation; default 0), tas_season and, per field, the database group(s) holding it (see Database).

Database

The snapshots bind varslice groups (see Database namelists) by name, from the par file itself if it has the group (searched implicitly first, so it never has to name itself and its groups win), else from the files listed in [forcing] var_defs, searched in order (first file that has the group). The snapshot groups [snapshot_<name>], the coefficients and the terms are looked up the same way. The shared database input/clim_db.nml holds one group per product field; a par file can add run-specific groups:

&forcing
    var_defs = "input/clim_db.nml" "input/forcing_db.nml"   ! the default
/
&my_tas                              ! in the par file: found before clim_db.nml
    filename = "ice_data/..."
    ...
/

A group missing from the par file and every var_defs file stops the run, naming the group and the files. The paths of the database take, besides {domain} and {grid_name}, two keywords:

  • {grid_global}: the global grid of the paleoclimate products, [snapshots] grid_global (default GLOBAL-1DEG);
  • {exp}: the experiment of the snapshot, its exp, so one group serves every experiment of a product family.

Present-day references are regional ({domain}/{grid_name}, the resolution of the climate grid): MAR v3.14 1961-1990 (mar314_1961_1990_{tas,sf,rf,pr,zs}), RACMO2.3p2 1981-2010 (racmo23p2_1981_2010_{tas,tsrf,pr,sf,zs}), ERA5 1981-2010 (era5_1981_2010_{tas,pr,sf,zs}), ORAS5 1981-2010 (oras5_1981_2010_{to,so}, monthly climatology) and the ISMIP7 ocean (ismip7_{to,so}). The paleoclimate snapshots are the global products, remapped online onto the climate grid (remap = "bilin", the map cached in maps/):

The Greenland reconstructions are regional products on GRL-16KM, remapped online (grid_src = "GRL-16KM") and filled from the nearest cell outside their box (fill = "nearest", e.g. on GRL-PAL):

The database also holds the groups of the transforms ([bias]: bias_mar_era5_1981_2010_{dtas,fpr}; [taper]: taper_b18_b20) and the ice histories of [ice_bnd] (paleomist_H_ice, glac1d_H_ice).

Time-varying groups. A group with a time axis (time = 1 x0 x1 dx nsub) is sliced at the forcing time time_rel at the start and at every climate update: linear interpolation between the two bracketing records, the first or last record outside the axis (varslice extrap). Its snapshot is then reduced to sea level again (and the reference reset, if it is the reference). A group without a time axis (time = 0 ...) is read once at the start.

&snapshot_pd
    tas = "mar314_1961_1990_tas"
    pr  = "mar314_1961_1990_sf" "mar314_1961_1990_rf"
    zs  = "mar314_1961_1990_zs"
    to  = "oras5_1981_2010_to"
    so  = "oras5_1981_2010_so"
/

&snapshot_lgm
    exp        = "lgm"
    monthly    = False
    clim_sigma = 50.0
    tas = "pmip3_mean_tas_ann" "pmip3_mean_tas_jja"
    pr  = "pmip3_mean_pr_ann"
    zs  = "pmip3_mean_zs"
/

Defaults

input/yelmox_defaults.nml declares every key of [snapshots] and [snapshot_<name>], of the forcing ([forcing], the coefficients, the terms) and of the transforms, with its default. The named groups fall back to a template group: [snapshot_<name>] to [snapshot], [snapshots<sfx>] to [snapshots] with the domain suffix of a multi-domain driver ([snapshots_north]). A par file lists only what differs; a key the defaults file does not declare stops the run (nml_validate), and scripts/check_par_nml.py reports it offline.

Fields and composition

Fields are optional per snapshot. A snapshot binds the fields it names (a key absent or "" binds nothing). The backend builds what the reference snapshot ([forcing] reference) binds: the atmosphere (tas and pr, with the elevation zs) and/or the ocean (to, optionally so). An ocean-only reference serves the ocean of the rembo climate; climate = "snapshots" needs the atmosphere. Checked at start-up, with an error naming the group:

  • every snapshot of a term binds the fields the term lists, and no snapshot binds a field the reference does not;
  • a snapshot with tas or pr binds zs (the sea-level reduction), except the snap1 of a term without snap0 (below); pr with zs needs tas; so needs to; the reference binds tas and pr together;
  • the number of groups: tas one (monthly = True) or two (annual mean and one season); pr one or two summed (monthly, e.g. snowfall + rainfall) or one (annual); every other field one.

Seasons. A snapshot with two tas groups (annual mean ta_ann and one season ta_seas) builds the monthly cycle as a cosine with its extremes in January and July, c(m) = cos(2π(m-1)/12): with tas_season = "djf", tas(m) = ta_ann + (ta_seas - ta_ann) c(m) (the season in January); with "jja", tas(m) = ta_ann - (ta_seas - ta_ann) c(m) (in July). The default is the summer of the hemisphere (jja north, djf south), so the second field of CLIMBER-3α (JJA only) serves the south as its winter, and PMIP3 offers both (t2m_jja, t2m_djf).

Ocean. to and so are depth profiles, interpolated to the model depth axis (23 levels, 0-3000 m). A monthly climatology (12 slices, e.g. ORAS5) is averaged to the annual mean; an annual field (one slice: no time axis, e.g. the CLIMBER ocean products, or one record) is taken as is. Without so, the salinity is 0.

Composition. Each term with the snapshots pattern adds to the reference with the weight a_k, the value of its coefficient; the rule is fixed by field:

field now
tas, to, so ref + sum_k a_k (snap1_k - snap0_k)
pr ref * prod_k (1 + a_k (snap1_k/snap0_k - 1))

A term with snap0 = "" adds its snap1 snapshot as the anomaly itself: ref + a_k snap1_k (tas) or ref (1 + a_k (snap1_k - 1)) (pr), e.g. a reconstructed temperature anomaly or precipitation ratio (Badgeley et al., 2020) without a zero snapshot. Such a snapshot binds no zs: its fields are taken as given, so they must be sea-level anomalies or ratios (there is no elevation correction of their own). If it binds zs, it is reduced to sea level like any other snapshot.

&term_b20                     ! input/forcing_db.nml
    snap0  = ""
    snap1  = "b20"            ! snapshot binding tas (anomaly) and pr (ratio), no zs
    fields = "pr"             ! coef = "": the constant 1
/

Two terms give the orbital + millennial decomposition of Banderas et al. (2018), e.g. lgm on picontrol -> lgm and millennial on lgm_weak -> lgm_strong, each on its own coefficient; several terms may share one coefficient:

&forcing
    reference = "pd"
    coefs     = "alpha" "beta"
    terms     = "lgm" "millennial"
/

&coef_alpha
    method      = "series"
    series_file = "input/alpha_orbital_r2020.dat"
    norm_t0     = 0.0             ! [yr] alpha = 0 here
    norm_t1     = -21e3           ! [yr] alpha = 1 here (or a window, e.g. -23e3 -19e3: the mean)
/

&coef_beta
    method      = "series"
    series_file = "input/beta_ngrip_millennial.dat"
/

&term_lgm
    coef   = "alpha"
    snap0  = "picontrol"
    snap1  = "lgm"
    fields = "tas" "pr"
/

&term_millennial
    coef   = "beta"
    snap0  = "lgm_weak"
    snap1  = "lgm_strong"
    fields = "tas" "pr"
/

Turning a proxy record into a coefficient is done by tsgen (norm_t0/norm_t1, see the fesm-utils tsgen docs); a steady state is a const coefficient (df_dt_max = 0, value f_min), independent of the model time.

Uniform terms (pattern = "uniform") add f_ta a to tas (and multiply pr by exp(β_p f_ta a)), f_to a to to and f_so a to so on the fields they list. The ocean anomaly from the atmosphere is a derived coefficient (method = "derived", from = "air_anomaly": factor times the mean sea-level air temperature anomaly of the same update over region, without lag and without the lapse-rate cooling of the ice sheet) with a uniform term on to, the library coef_dTo + term_ocean; to without terms stays at the reference. The ocean terms are applied after the atmosphere (a derived coefficient may drive ocean fields only).

The backend holds no state of its own: the coefficients restart with the driver (coef_<name>_restart.nc). The climate output carries the value of each term’s coefficient, term_<name>.

The transient forcing is added on top in every configuration.
